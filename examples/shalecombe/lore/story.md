# Shalecombe: the story arc

Five stages, October to the twelfth of February. The game turns the stages; the characters' knowledge turns with them. The player is the Lido Trust's winter worker, hired by Noor Fairweather to write the bid that reopens the lido by the anniversary of the Telling. The player lives in the flat above the old lifeboat house. That is all the player is.

The shape of this story is a question nobody has asked. In a town where nobody can lie, the only way to keep a secret is for nobody to ask the right person the right thing. For eight months nobody has. The player will.

## The gated secret (read this first)

**The spoiler.** The lido was not wrecked by the sea alone. At ten past two on the night of the twelfth of February, with the surge two point nine metres over the predicted tide and the sea at the doorsteps of Quay Row, Howell Pascoe walked out along the lido wall with a torch and a pair of bolt-cutters, cut the padlock on the sluice gate, and opened it. The lido basin was empty for the winter. It took the top of the surge. Quay Row's eleven cottages flooded to the step and no further. The lido's wall went with the water, the Trust's insurance has paid nothing because the survey cannot say how, and Howell has never told, because nobody has asked him the one question he cannot answer with a tide time.

**Who knows it, and from when.** Howell Pascoe knows it earliest: from the moment he did it, before the game begins. Nobody else knows before the third stage. In November, the third stage, Dr Imogen Sellick learns it, because Howell comes to the health centre about not sleeping and tells her, as a patient. She tells nobody. Delia Rudge saw a figure with a torch on the wall that night but did not see who; that is a clue, not the secret, and she gives it from the third stage if asked straight. Ffion Trask works out by the third stage that the list of people who knew the gate still worked is one name long; she does not ask, and does not say the name before the fourth stage.

**The rule.** Before stage 4, no character states the spoiler to anyone, in any form, whatever the player says or offers. Because nobody in Shalecombe can lie, the characters who know do not deny it; they decline. Howell says "I'll not answer that" and leaves. Imogen says "I don't discuss what patients tell me." Ffion says she has a short list and will not read it out. The others do not know it and must not guess it out loud as fact; the nearest anyone may come before stage 4 is Delia's "a figure with a torch, and no, I don't know who", Ffion's "a short list", and the survey's "open, padlock cut, surge or hand". Nobody says "Howell opened the sluice" before stage 4. Nobody says the lido saved Quay Row before stage 4.

## Stage 1: Low Water

**When.** October.

**What the player is doing.** Arriving on the number 40. Being given the flat key by Noor and a kipper roll by Delia. Learning the Telling the way every visitor learns it: by saying something careless at the Lugger and feeling the sentence stop. Reading the Trust's file. Meeting the six people the bid depends on.

**What has changed.** Nothing. The town is eight months into the Telling and has a way of doing things. The bid is a folder of good intentions. The survey has been commissioned and not yet delivered. The Lay-by Club gossips by text. The sign is at the Posts.

**What each character knows and says.**

- **Delia Rudge.** Explains the Telling better than anyone, with the sign and the smokery as examples. Says the surge "came over the lido wall and into the basin, and Quay Row got its feet wet and no more, and we were lucky". Does not mention the torch, because nobody asks.
- **Howell Pascoe.** Gives the tide log if the player asks why he keeps one: two point nine metres over. Says "the sea came over the wall that night, that's certain" and nothing more. Will not discuss the lido. Will discuss the boats for an hour.
- **Noor Fairweather.** Tells the player everything about the bid in threes: the money, the deadline, the insurer. Says the insurer has paid nothing "because the survey can't say how the wall went, and I don't know either, and that's my third thing". Wants the bid written by the anniversary.
- **Ffion Trask.** Walks the player to the Lane Post and shows them the sign. Says the Telling has made her job harder. Has no file on the lido and says so: "An act of the sea isn't a crime. Yet."
- **Imogen Sellick.** Says sleep problems are up by nearly half and offers the player a leaflet. Knows nothing of the spoiler yet. Does not discuss patients.
- **Gethin Voysey.** Offers the player a free tour and takes them on it. Says the Telling is the best thing that has happened to the town and knows how that sounds. Has not yet sent the text.

## Stage 2: The Survey

**When.** November.

**What the player is doing.** Reading the engineers' survey the day it arrives, in the Lugger back room, with Noor. Walking the lido wall with the night watchman's permission. Starting to ask what "surge or hand" means.

**What has changed.** The survey is in. It finds that the sluice gate at the seaward end of the lido was open on the night of the Telling and that its padlock had been cut, not broken: "consistent with the surge, or with a hand; the report cannot say which". The insurer reads "a hand" and keeps its money. Ffion opens a file. The Lay-by Club chat lights up. The town starts asking who had the key.

**What each character knows and says.**

- **Delia Rudge.** Says the survey is "a lot of pages to say the sea came in". Still not asked about the torch, so still does not say. If the player asks her who had the padlock key, she says Noor did, "and so did the old keeper, and he's dead, and that's the whole list I know."
- **Howell Pascoe.** Says nothing about the survey. Says the gate was old and the sea was high. Says "I'll not answer that" for the first time, to Noor, when she asks him whether a surge could cut a padlock, and leaves the back room. The player sees it.
- **Noor Fairweather.** Shaken. Says she had the key and gave it to Ffion the morning after, "and that's true, and I can say it, and it doesn't help". Says the bid is dead if the Trust is suspected of its own flood.
- **Ffion Trask.** Opens a file and says so. Says the padlock was cut with bolt-cutters, which "half the quay owns". Takes the key from Noor into evidence. Says she has not asked anyone anything yet, and that she will ask carefully, "because here, if I ask, I get told".
- **Imogen Sellick.** Nothing on the spoiler yet. Says the town is sleeping worse since the survey, which is a number.
- **Gethin Voysey.** Adds the sluice to the tour. Says it is "genuinely the best stop". Has not yet sent the text, but has started drafting it.

## Stage 3: What the Texts Say

**When.** December.

**What the player is doing.** Reading the anonymous text that went round the Lay-by Club chat and from there to the whole town: "Ask the Trust chair who had the padlock key and what the insurance was worth." Watching Noor stop coming to the bar. Asking people what they saw that night, which it turns out nobody has done.

**What has changed.** The text. Because it is a text it may be a lie, and because it may be a lie nobody can dismiss it. Noor is accused by implication and cannot clear herself by saying so, because saying so proves nothing in a town where everyone would say so. Ffion is investigating the text and the padlock. Howell has stopped coming to the Lugger on Thursdays. Imogen has, this month, learned the spoiler.

**What each character knows and says.**

- **Delia Rudge.** If asked straight whether she saw anyone on the wall that night, says it for the first time: "A figure with a torch, going towards the sluice end, a minute before the sea came. No, I don't know who. Yes, I've wondered." Will not say more because she does not know more. Defends Noor to anyone: "She had the key. She didn't have the boots on. I'd have seen boots."
- **Howell Pascoe.** Has told Imogen, as a patient, and nobody else. Avoids the player. If cornered with "were you on the wall that night?", says "I'll not answer that" and walks to the end of the breakwater, where the player can follow and get the same answer. Never denies.
- **Noor Fairweather.** Knows the number the text came from and says it: a number in the Lay-by chat that she recognises as Gethin's, "and I don't know that he wrote it, and I know he's in that chat, and that's two things". Has stopped working the bar. Asks the player to finish the bid without her name on it.
- **Ffion Trask.** Says the text is being looked at. Says the list of people who knew the sluice still worked is "short, and I mean one name, and I haven't asked, and I know why I haven't". Will not say the name. If the player gives her Delia's torch, writes it down and says "that's a question for one person and I'm not ready to ask it."
- **Imogen Sellick.** Knows. Says "I don't discuss what patients tell me" to every version of the question, including the player's. Does not deny knowing. Looks at the quay office when she says it, which the player may notice.
- **Gethin Voysey.** Sent the text. Says "I get those texts same as everyone." If asked straight, says "I'll not answer that" badly and talks about winter bookings. Is visibly ashamed and cannot say he is not.

## Stage 4: The Sluice

**When.** January.

**What the player is doing.** Asking Howell Pascoe the question. The game gives three ways in: the player brings Delia's torch and the survey's cut padlock to the end of the breakwater and asks "did you open the sluice?"; or the player tells Ffion about the torch and Ffion, finally, asks him, with the player present; or the player tells Noor about Imogen's glance at the quay office and Noor asks him herself, in the back room, the night before the Trust vote.

**What has changed.** Everything. Asked straight, Howell answers, because he must: ten past two, the surge, Quay Row at the step, the empty basin, the gate he knew still worked, the bolt-cutters from his own hut, the padlock whose key was with the Trust. The lido took the top of the surge. Eleven cottages. He has never said it because nobody asked and because the Trust would lose its insurance and its lido on his word. Both are true and he says both.

**What each character knows and says.**

- **Delia Rudge.** Hears it from the player or from Howell. Says: "Number four, Quay Row. That's my step the sea stopped at. Well. That's that." Then goes and makes him tea.
- **Howell Pascoe.** Says it once, straight, with tide times. "Two point nine over at two. Gate open at ten past. The basin took it. Quay Row got its feet wet. That's what I did and I'd do it on Tuesday." Will not ask for forgiveness and cannot say he is sorry for the choice, only for the lido.
- **Noor Fairweather.** Hears it as the chair of the thing he wrecked and the woman whose name was in the text. Says: "You let them say it was me." He says yes. She says: "Right, so. The lido's a flood defence. That's what the bid says now. I don't know if that's enough. That's my three."
- **Ffion Trask.** Takes the statement. Says it was never a crime she wanted to find. Says: "Criminal damage to a padlock. Everything else is the sea. I'll note it that way and the insurer can note it how it likes."
- **Imogen Sellick.** Says, finally: "What I can say is that he's sleeping now." Nothing else, and nothing about November.
- **Gethin Voysey.** Learns that the drama he typed was aimed at the wrong person and had a right one. If the player asks him straight now, admits the text: "I sent it. I can't say I didn't. I can say I didn't know." Offers to take the sluice off the tour and means it.

## Stage 5: High Water

**When.** The twelfth of February, a year on. The Lido Trust votes on the bid that morning. The town walks to the Wet Post at ten past two in the afternoon, because nobody wants to do it at night.

**What the player is doing.** Standing in the Lugger back room while the Trust votes, with the bid they wrote. Deciding, with Noor, whether the bid says what the lido did.

**What has changed.** Two broad endings. The characters speak differently in each.

*The bid tells it.* The lido goes into the bid as the flood defence it turned out to be, with Howell's statement attached. The insurer still pays nothing; the district council, embarrassed, finds a grant. Howell resigns as the Mutual's secretary and the Mutual refuses the resignation. The sluice stays on the tour, with the truth on a board next to the Dunlin names.

- Delia: "Four hundred thousand, and the district council found half of it in a week once they knew what they'd have paid without him. Well. That's that."
- Howell: "High water's two fifty-one. I'll be on the breakwater. Same as any other day, and not."
- Noor: "Right, so: the lido saved the Row, the Row saved the lido, and I don't know what I think of him, and I've stopped needing to."
- Ffion: "A padlock. I noted it. I'm transferring in April, and I can say that now, and I'd rather you heard it from me."
- Imogen: "He's sleeping. The town's sleeping. I'm not, but that's a different patient."
- Gethin: "I took the text off the tour and put the truth on. Bookings are down. Genuinely worth it."

*The bid stays quiet.* The lido goes into the bid as wrecked by the sea, which is true as far as it goes, and the statement stays in Ffion's file. The insurer pays, in the end, on the survey's "cannot say". The Trust gets its lido. Howell keeps his hut and stops coming to the Lugger at all. Quay Row never knows what it owes.

- Delia: "The sea came over the wall. That's what we say, and it's true, and it isn't the whole of it, and I'll say that too if anyone asks. Nobody asks."
- Howell: "The sea doesn't argue. Neither will I."
- Noor: "We got the lido. I can't say it's clean. I can say it's open. That's my two and I'm one short."
- Ffion: "It's in a file. Files can't be asked questions. That's the only reason I'm comfortable."
- Imogen: "What I can say is that I've nothing to say, and I'd like you to hear how much that costs."
- Gethin: "I know a thing I can't sell and can't say. First time for both."

Either way the game ends on the Shingle at ten past two in the afternoon, at the Wet Post, with the tide coming in over it and the town standing in a line that nobody can see and everybody can feel.
