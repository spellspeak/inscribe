# Shalecombe: world bible

Working draft 5. Shalecombe is a real-feeling town in the real world, now, with one thing wrong with it. Everything in this document is either ordinary and true of any small seaside town, or it is about the one thing. We have tried to keep the two apart so writers can see which is which.

## The pitch in one breath

A small town on the south-west coast, three thousand people, five fishing boats, a quay, a shut-up lido, a pub, a kipper smokery and a bus that goes to the next town once an hour. Since one night last winter nobody inside the parish boundary can tell a lie out loud. Everybody knows it. The outside world half believes it. The town has had eight months to get used to it and has not.

The player arrives in October as the Lido Trust's winter worker, hired to write the bid that reopens the lido, and finds that the one question nobody in Shalecombe has asked is the one the town is built around.

## Setting

Present day, this coast, this country. Phones work, cars work, the internet is slow but there. Card payments, the news, football on the telly, the weather app, the price of diesel. Shalecombe is poor in the way fishing towns are poor and pretty in the way they are pretty: a stone quay from 1874 with a row of eleven cottages behind it, a steep lane up to the main road, a shingle beach to the east with a seawater lido at its end, and a rock offshore called the Thrum with an automatic light on it. The next town, Penmorrow, is eleven miles up the coast and has the hospital, the secondary school, the supermarket and the fish auction. Shalecombe has what is in this document.

We never name a real brand, a real chain, a real network, a real app or a real newspaper. Characters say "my phone", "the supermarket in Penmorrow", "the streaming thing", "the paper in Penmorrow". Every business in Shalecombe is invented and named here.

## The Telling (this is the heart of the game, players must hear all of it)

On the night of Thursday the twelfth of February, this year, at about ten past two in the morning, a storm surge came over the lido wall, and from that moment nobody inside the Shalecombe parish boundary has been able to say a thing they believe to be untrue. The town calls it the Telling. The date is to be said exactly: the night of the twelfth of February. *Say it exactly.*

How it works, as far as anyone can tell, and the writers must keep these straight:

- It applies to speech. If you open your mouth to say a thing you believe false, the words do not come. People describe it as a cough that eats the sentence, or a dry mouth that only wets for the true thing. You can stop. You can say nothing. You can say "I'll not answer that." *Players must hear this.*
- It does not apply to writing. Texts, notes, emails, the comments under the Penmorrow paper's website: all of it can lie, and does. The town has learned that anything typed may be false and anything spoken is true. Half the town's rows now happen by text for exactly that reason. *Players must hear this.*
- It applies to anyone inside the line, resident or visitor, from the moment they cross it. The line is the old parish boundary, marked by three stones: the Top Post on the Penmorrow road, the Lane Post at the top of Combe Lane, and the Wet Post at the east end of the Shingle, which is under water at high tide. Fishermen say the line runs from the Wet Post straight out to the Thrum, and the boats' radio chat inside that line has been honest since February. *Players must hear where the line is.*
- Being wrong is fine. The Telling stops lies, not mistakes. You can say the bus is at ten past when it is at twenty past, if you believe it.
- Opinions are fine. Questions are fine. Promises are fine, because a promise is about what you mean to do. Stories are fine if everyone knows they are stories: "once upon a time" works, "I swear this happened" does not unless it did.
- Sarcasm comes out flat. People have mostly stopped trying. Jokes that need a false claim get stuck halfway; puns are safe. *Background colour, but writers will want it.*
- Nobody knows why. There are three theories in the town and the game never picks one. The cable theory: the Marcher cable, an undersea data cable, came ashore in the bay the week before. The sea theory: the surge came over the lido wall that night and something came with it. Delia Rudge's theory: it was about time. Characters may have a favourite. Characters never claim to know. *Players must hear that nobody knows.*
- The player is inside the line too. If the player says something a character knows to be false, the character treats it as a mistake, never a lie: "You're wrong about that. You can't be lying, so you're wrong." *Players must hear this, because it is how the town will treat them.*

What it has done to the town, in eight months: the amateur dramatics folded in March. The Penmorrow paper ran the headline "The Town That Can't Lie" in September and the town hates it. Shalecombe Honesty Tours started in May and charges fifteen pounds. The Lay-by Club meets on Tuesday nights in the lay-by past the Top Post, outside the line, to play cards and lie to each other for the relief of it. The health centre says sleep problems are up by nearly half. The primary school has a quiet corner for children who need to not answer. A documentary crew came in March and left after two days when the presenter could not do the pieces to camera. The parish put a sign up at each Post in April, and the sign reads exactly: "You are entering Shalecombe. Please speak carefully." *Say the sign exactly.*

## History (eight to twelve things, dated)

1. **1874.** The stone quay is finished. The plaque on the quay head carries the town's motto, which is to be said exactly: "Built by the sea, for the sea, against the sea." *Say it exactly.*
2. **1911.** The lido opens at the east end of the Shingle: a seawater pool behind a sea wall, filled and emptied by a sluice gate at the seaward end. For a hundred years it is the thing the town is known for. *Players must hear what the lido is.*
3. **1953.** The Shalecombe lifeboat takes nineteen people off the coaster Dunlin, aground on the Thrum. The names are on a board in the old lifeboat house. *Background colour.*
4. **1979.** The fish auction moves to Penmorrow. Shalecombe's fleet goes from thirty boats to twelve inside five years. *Background colour.*
5. **1998.** Annie Rudge opens the Kipper House on the quay. Her daughter Delia runs it now. *Background colour.*
6. **2007.** The lifeboat moves to Penmorrow. The lifeboat house on the quay closes. Howell Pascoe, who had crewed it since 1989, becomes quaymaster the same year. *Players must hear that the lifeboat is gone.*
7. **2016.** A January storm takes the outer third of the breakwater. It has never been rebuilt. *Background colour.*
8. **2023.** The district council hands the lido, shut since 2019, to the newly formed Lido Trust for one pound, on condition it reopens by the end of 2027. *Players must hear this: it is the deadline the story runs on.*
9. **The twelfth of February, this year.** The Telling. The surge comes over the lido wall at ten past two and into the basin; Quay Row floods to the doorsteps and no further. By morning nobody in the town can lie. *Say the date exactly.*
10. **March.** The documentary crew. Two days. *Background colour.*
11. **May.** The first Honesty Tour leaves from the old lifeboat house. **June.** The Lay-by Club starts meeting. *Background colour.*
12. **October, this year.** Now. The Lido Trust hires a winter worker, the player, to write the reopening bid by the anniversary.

## Geography: the places

**The Quay.** Stone, 1874, the plaque at its head, five boats on it, the fish auction shed (empty since 1979, used for ice and nets), the quay office, and the Kipper House. Cars are kept off it from six in the evening. *Players must hear that the quay is the centre of the town.*

**Quay Row.** Eleven cottages behind the quay, below the road, the lowest houses in the town. They flooded to the step on the night of the Telling. Delia Rudge lives at number four. *Players must hear the eleven cottages.*

**The Kipper House.** Smokery and café on the quay. Delia Rudge. The sign says "Smoked this morning", and since February it has had to be true, so some mornings the sign is turned to the wall.

**The Lugger.** The pub, at the bottom of Combe Lane. Noor Fairweather. Quiz on Thursdays. A room upstairs. The Lido Trust meets in the back room on the first Monday of the month.

**The Lido.** East end of the Shingle. A seawater pool behind a sea wall, 1911, fenced off since 2019, wrecked on the night of the Telling when the surge came over the wall. A contractor from Penmorrow has a survey cabin and a night watchman on the site. The sluice gate is at the seaward end. *Players must hear what state it is in.*

**The Shingle.** The beach between the quay and the lido. Steep, loud, no sand. The Shingle Dippers swim from it at seven every morning, whatever the month. Spring tides cover it to the sea wall, which catches a visitor most years. *Players must hear the tide warning.*

**The Breakwater and the Thrum.** The breakwater runs out from the quay, two-thirds of what it was. The Thrum is the rock half a mile off with the automatic light, which flashes once every six seconds. Howell Pascoe stands at the end of the breakwater at the turn of every tide.

**The Blue Door.** The cottage on Combe Lane with the blue door, which is where Sergeant Ffion Trask and two constables work. Everyone calls it the Blue Door. Nobody calls it anything else.

**The health centre.** Combe Lane, next to the Blue Door. Dr Imogen Sellick, four days a week; the other doctor is in Penmorrow on Tuesdays.

**The old lifeboat house.** On the quay. The Mutual owns it. Shalecombe Honesty Tours has the ground floor, the Dunlin board is still on the wall, and the flat upstairs is let to the Lido Trust for its winter worker, which is where the player sleeps.

**Seaward Primary.** The primary school at the top of Combe Lane, by the Lane Post. Ninety children. The quiet corner.

**The three Posts.** The Top Post on the Penmorrow road, the Lane Post by Seaward Primary, the Wet Post on the Shingle. The line of the Telling. Each has the sign.

**Penmorrow.** Off the map, eleven miles up the coast. Hospital, secondary school, supermarket, fish auction, the paper, the lifeboat. The number 40 bus goes there once an hour from the top of Combe Lane.

## Factions

**The Lido Trust.** Two hundred and forty members, chaired by Noor Fairweather. Owns the lido for a pound. Has raised sixty-one thousand pounds of the four hundred thousand it needs to reopen by the end of 2027. Its insurer has paid nothing for the night of the Telling, because the survey cannot say how the wall was breached. The Trust employs the player. *Players must hear the numbers: sixty-one thousand raised, four hundred thousand needed.*

**The Mutual.** The Shalecombe Boat Owners' Mutual: five boats, the Kittiwake, the Sprat, the Fair Chance, the Second Wind and the Tregenna, and their crews. Owns the lifeboat house. Howell Pascoe is its secretary. It is the oldest thing in the town that still works. *Players must hear there are five boats.*

**The Lay-by Club.** About thirty people who drive out past the Top Post on Tuesday nights to a lay-by on the Penmorrow road, outside the line, and play cards and tell each other lies for the pleasure of it. They have a group chat, which is where most of the town's rumours now start, because a group chat can lie. Sergeant Trask is certain they are planning something. They are not. *Players must hear what it is.*

**Shalecombe Says.** The trade association of the truth-tourism businesses: Gethin Voysey's Honesty Tours, two bed-and-breakfasts, and the Lugger, which joined reluctantly for the quiz custom. It is Gethin's idea and Gethin's name for it and most of the town pretends it does not exist. *Background colour.*

## Rules of the world: what is and is not possible

- This is the real world, now. Everything true outside Shalecombe is true inside it, except the one thing.
- Nobody inside the line can say what they believe to be false. Nobody has ever managed it, not once, not for a dare, not drunk. *Players must hear this.*
- Writing can lie. The town knows it and trusts nothing it reads from a Shalecombe number.
- Nobody knows why, and nobody in the town will claim to. A character who says they know why is wrong, and can only say it if they believe it, so they are the sort of person who believes things.
- The Telling is never explained in play and never lifts. There is no cure, no ritual, no scientist with an answer. The documentary crew got nothing. *Players must hear this if they test it.*
- Everything else is ordinary. Cars go. Phones ring. The bus is late. The tide comes in. Spring tides cover the Shingle to the sea wall.
- The quay is closed to cars from six in the evening. The lido is fenced and the fence is alarmed.
- The Thrum light flashes once every six seconds. Boats inside the line keep their radio honest.

## The boundary: what the characters know of the real world

Everything. Shalecombe is the real world with one rule added. Characters know what a phone is, what the internet is, what the news said, who won on Saturday, what a mortgage is, what Father Christmas is, and that the children of Shalecombe have had to be told the truth about him.

Denied, because the Telling makes them impossible inside the line: telling a lie out loud, bluffing at cards, surprise parties, sales patter, acting a part you do not believe, and a spoken alibi. If a player asks a character to do one of these, the character cannot, and says so, or says nothing. Characters do not pretend to try.

Denied, because this is a game and not an advert: real brand names, real chains, real networks, real apps, real newspapers, real people. A character who needs one says "my phone", "the supermarket in Penmorrow", "the streaming thing", "the paper in Penmorrow". The player can say what they like; the characters do not repeat it.

## Tone notes for writers

Nobody in Shalecombe is amazed by the Telling any more. They are tired of it, or fond of it, or making a living off it. It comes up in conversation the way the weather does. The jokes are small and mostly about texting.

People say "I don't know" a great deal, because it is often the only true thing available. They say "I'll not answer that" and it is not rude. Silence is a sentence here.

Keep it warm. This is not a horror story. It is a town that has had to find out what it was built on, and found that it was built on a man who will not answer a question.
