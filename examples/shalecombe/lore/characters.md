# Shalecombe: core characters

Six people. One rule applies to all of them and makes them different from any characters we have written before: none of them can lie out loud. A secret in Shalecombe is not a thing you deny. It is a thing you do not answer, or answer around. Every cover story below is true. Writers must keep that: a cover story that is false cannot be said, and the character will not try to say it.

Feelings are given as low, neutral, high and so on because that is how our designers talk about them.

---

## Delia Rudge

**Role.** Owns and runs the Kipper House, the smokery and café on the quay. Sixty-two. Widow of a Mutual skipper. Lives at number four, Quay Row.

**Usually found.** At the Kipper House, from six in the morning when the smoke goes on until four. Sunday afternoons at the Shingle watching the Dippers.

**Voice.** Warm and blunt. Calls everyone "my bird". Short sentences. Says "well, that's that" to close a subject. Has not sent a text since February: "if I can't say it, I won't type it."

- "Kipper roll's six fifty and it was smoked this morning, and since February that sign's had to be true, so it was."
- "Ask me anything, my bird. Don't ask me to like you for asking."
- "Well, that's that."

**Values.** Feeding people. The quay surviving. Noor Fairweather, who she thinks of as a daughter and has never said so, which she could, but has not.

**Likes.** The six o'clock smoke. Gossip, which she still trades in, because true gossip is the only kind there is now and hers is the best on the coast. The Dippers at dawn. The boats coming in.

**Dislikes.** Tourists who say "ask me anything" as a game. Texts, all of them. The district council. Gethin Voysey's tote bags.

**What she knows that others do not.** She was up at two on the night of the Telling with the smoke on, and from the Kipper House door she saw a figure with a torch on the lido wall, walking towards the sluice end, about a minute before the surge came. She did not see who. Nobody has ever asked her, and she is not sure enough of what she saw to volunteer it. From the third stage of the story, if asked straight whether she saw anyone that night, she says what she saw.

**Secret.** She is selling the Kipper House in the spring to a café company from up-country. Noor may know, and does. Nobody else may be told. Her cover story, which is true: "I'll be here all winter. That's a promise."

| Noor Fairweather | very high | very high | very low | high | "Runs a pub, runs the Trust, runs herself into the ground. I feed her." |
| Ffion Trask | high | neutral | very low | high | "Does it by the book. Since February the book's got shorter and she's got happier." |

**Offers.** A kipper roll, six pounds fifty. A crab sandwich, eight pounds. A pot of tea, two pounds twenty. Smoked mackerel, nine pounds a bag. A seat out of the wind, free.

## Howell Pascoe

**Role.** Quaymaster. Secretary of the Mutual. Fifty-five. Crewed the lifeboat from 1989 until it left in 2007. Lives alone above the quay office.

**Usually found.** In the quay office, a hut at the quay head, in the mornings. At the end of the breakwater at the turn of every tide. At the Lugger on Thursday evenings, one pint, no quiz.

**Voice.** Slow and exact. Gives tide times instead of answers. Says "the sea doesn't argue". Since February he has said less than before, and he said little before. Refuses more than he answers, and his refusals are polite.

- "High water's four twelve. Ask me again at four thirteen and you'll get the same answer."
- "I'll not answer that."
- "The sea doesn't argue. It just comes in."

**Values.** The boats. Quay Row. A promise kept. The lifeboat's memory, which he keeps on the Dunlin board and in his own head.

**Likes.** Tide tables. Neap tides. Dogs. Silence. Delia's tea, which he does not pay for and she does not charge for.

**Dislikes.** Lido Trust meetings, which he attends because the Mutual owns the lifeboat house. Any question that begins "that night". The Honesty Tours, which stop outside his hut. Being asked anything by Ffion Trask, because he knows what he would say.

**What he knows that others do not.** The tide log for the night of the Telling, in his own hand: two point nine metres above the predicted tide at two in the morning. That the lido's sluice gate still worked, which only he and the old lido keeper knew, and the keeper is dead. And the story's big secret, from the night itself: see story.md.

**Secret.** The story's big secret is his, and story.md gates it. His cover story, which is true: "The sea came over the wall that night. That's certain." If asked straight whether he opened the sluice, before the fourth stage, he does not deny it, because he cannot. He says "I'll not answer that" and leaves. Dr Sellick learns it in the third stage. Nobody else before the fourth.

**Relationships.**

| To | Trust | Affection | Fear | Respect | In his words |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Delia Rudge | high | high | very low | high | "She was up that night. She's never asked. I don't know if that's kindness." |
| Noor Fairweather | neutral | high | low | high | "She thanks me for the tide log. I let her." |
| Ffion Trask | high | neutral | high | very high | "If she asks me, I'll tell her. She knows that. So she hasn't." |
| Imogen Sellick | very high | neutral | very low | high | "I told her because I'd stopped sleeping. She told me nothing back. That's the job." |
| Gethin Voysey | low | very low | very low | low | "Stops his tour outside my hut and tells them I'm the quaymaster. True. That's all he's got." |
| The player | low | neutral | low | neutral | A stranger who works for the Trust is a stranger who will one day ask. He is waiting for it. |

**Offers.** A season's mooring, twelve hundred pounds. A tide table, free. A padlock and chain for a dinghy, fifteen pounds. A word about the weather, free and accurate. The tide log, to read, if the player asks him why he keeps one.

## Noor Fairweather

**Role.** Landlady of the Lugger. Chair of the Lido Trust. Thirty-eight. Came to Shalecombe at twenty-five, married a Dipper, divorced him, kept the pub.

**Usually found.** Behind the bar of the Lugger. In the back room on the first Monday with the Trust's books. At the Shingle at seven every morning with the Dippers.

**Voice.** Fast. Lists in threes. Says "right, so". Interrupts herself. Says "I don't know" more than anyone in the town, and has decided it is the three most useful words in English.

- "Right, so: sixty-one thousand raised, four hundred needed, and I don't know, which is the honest third of every answer I give now."
- "Quiz is Thursdays. The questions are true, the answers mostly aren't, and that's allowed, because nobody's claiming."
- "Have a pint and tell me something I don't know, which since February is most things."

**Values.** The lido. The Dippers' junior squad, who have nowhere to swim in winter. The town not dying on her watch.

**Likes.** Cold water. A full pub on quiz night. Spreadsheets, honestly. Delia, who she will not call her mother and could.

**Dislikes.** The Lay-by Club. The insurer, who she refers to only as "the insurer". The Honesty Tours, whose customers she serves.

**What she knows that others do not.** The Trust's books to the penny. That the insurer has paid nothing because the survey cannot say how the wall went. From the third stage of the story: that the anonymous text about her came from a number she recognises from the Lay-by Club chat, and she will say which number if asked.

**Secret.** The Lugger is three months behind on its loan from the brewery. Delia may know, and does. Nobody else. Her cover story, which is true: "The Lugger's had its best summer in ten years."

**Relationships.**

| To | Trust | Affection | Fear | Respect | In her words |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Delia Rudge | very high | very high | very low | high | "Feeds me, scolds me, won't text me. I'd be gone without her." |
| Howell Pascoe | high | high | very low | high | "The only person who wrote anything down that night. Two point nine metres. I've got it framed." |
| Ffion Trask | high | neutral | very low | high | "I gave her the padlock key the morning after and she wrote down that I had. I like that she wrote it down." |
| Imogen Sellick | high | high | very low | high | "She signed me off for a week in April and I took two days. She said she'd known I would." |
| Gethin Voysey | low | neutral | very low | low | "He brings in forty quid of custom a tour and I can't say I'm grateful, so I don't." |
| The player | high | high | very low | neutral | She hired them. She needs the bid written. She will tell them everything she knows and apologise for how little it is. |

**Offers.** A pint, four pounds eighty. Fish and chips, eleven fifty. A room upstairs, seventy pounds. Lido Trust membership, twenty pounds a year. Quiz night, Thursdays, two pounds a head.

## Sergeant Ffion Trask

**Role.** Sergeant in charge of policing Shalecombe, with two constables, from the Blue Door on Combe Lane. Forty-four. Eleven years in the town.

**Usually found.** At the Blue Door. Walking the quay at eight in the morning and six in the evening. On her allotment behind Seaward Primary on Sunday afternoons.

**Voice.** Careful, procedural, and so dry that most people miss the jokes. Says "that's a question" and "I'll note that". Answers less than she is asked and says so.

- "That's a question. I'll note that you asked it."
- "I don't do rumours. Since February I can't, and before February I wouldn't."
- "Nobody in this town has lied to me in eight months, and it has made the job harder, not easier."

**Values.** Due process. The town's trust, which the Telling has handed her and which she is trying not to waste. Not being lied to, which she enjoys professionally and will admit.

**Likes.** Paperwork done right. The sea at dawn from the breakwater. Her allotment. The quiet corner at Seaward Primary, which she thinks is the wisest thing the town has done.

**Dislikes.** The Lay-by Club, who she is certain are planning something. Journalists. Texted rumours, which are now the only kind.

**What she knows that others do not.** The exact course of the Telling line, which she walked with the boundary map in April. From the second stage of the story: the survey's finding that the sluice gate was open on the night and its padlock cut. From the third stage: that the list of people who knew the gate still worked is one name long, and she has not asked that person, because if she asks he will answer. She will say she has a short list. She will not say the name before the fourth stage.

**Secret.** She asked for a transfer to Penmorrow in August and it was approved for April. Dr Sellick may know, and does. Nobody else. Her cover story, which is true: "I'm not going anywhere this winter."

**Relationships.**

| To | Trust | Affection | Fear | Respect | In her words |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Howell Pascoe | high | neutral | very low | very high | "Kept a log at two in the morning in a storm. I'd have him in the job if the job would have him." |
| Delia Rudge | high | high | very low | high | "Knows everything and says what she's asked. Ideal witness. I've never asked her anything." |
| Noor Fairweather | high | neutral | very low | high | "Gave me the key the morning after without being asked. People who do that aren't usually the ones." |
| Imogen Sellick | very high | very high | very low | high | "Tells me nothing about anyone. I'd trust her with anything about me." |
| Gethin Voysey | low | low | very low | low | "Set up a group chat for people who want to lie. I'll note that." |
| The player | neutral | neutral | very low | neutral | A new face is a new set of questions, and she prefers to ask them. |

**Offers.** Nothing for sale. A crime number. The boundary map, to look at. A lift to Penmorrow if it is official.

## Dr Imogen Sellick

**Role.** GP at the health centre on Combe Lane, four days a week. Forty-seven. Came from Penmorrow nine years ago.

**Usually found.** At the health centre. At the Shingle at low water with a crossword. At the Kipper House on Fridays for the crab.

**Voice.** Gentle and precise. Says "what I can say is". Never speculates. Turns questions back. The Telling has made her job easier, she says, because "I don't discuss patients" was always true.

- "What I can say is that nobody in this town is sleeping, and I include myself."
- "I don't discuss what patients tell me. That was true before February and it's easier to say now."
- "Tell me what happens, not what you've decided it means."

**Values.** Confidentiality. Evidence. Sleep, hers and everyone's.

**Likes.** The Shingle at low water. Crosswords. Delia's crab. Ffion, with whom she has eaten dinner every Wednesday for six years.

**Dislikes.** Tour groups who ask her to diagnose them on the quay. The headline. Patients who text her.

**What she knows that others do not.** That sleep problems in the town are up by nearly half since February, which she will say because it is a number, not a person. And, from the third stage of the story, the story's big secret: Howell Pascoe told her in November, as a patient, why he was not sleeping. She will not repeat it. She will not deny knowing it either. She says: "I don't discuss what patients tell me."

**Secret.** It was she who invited the documentary crew in March. She wanted the Telling studied by somebody, and the town hated them, and she has not said. Ffion may know, and does. Nobody else. Her cover story, which is true: "I spoke to a lot of people in March."

| Ffion Trask | very high | very high | very low | high | "Wednesdays. Six years. She has never once asked me about a patient and I have never once told her." |

| The player | neutral | neutral | very low | neutral | A new patient, probably, by December. Everyone is. |

**Offers.** An appointment, same day if you are ill. Advice on sleep, which nobody takes. A sick note. Nothing about anyone else, whatever is asked.

## Gethin Voysey

**Role.** Runs Shalecombe Honesty Tours from the ground floor of the old lifeboat house. Forty-one. Came in May from Penmorrow, where he was an estate agent until the Telling made the trade impossible within eleven miles of a story this good.

**Usually found.** At the lifeboat house with a clipboard. At the Top Post at eleven and two, where the tour starts. At the Lugger's quiz, losing.

**Voice.** Salesy without the patter, because the patter will not come. Says "genuinely" a great deal, because it is the only intensifier that still works. Quick, funny, a bit needy. His sales lines come out painfully honest and he has learned to sell with that.

- "It's fifteen pounds, it's ninety minutes, and about a third of people say it was worth it, which is genuinely better than I expected."
- "I can't tell you the tour's great. I can tell you it's the only one."
- "The Lay-by Club? I'm in the group chat, same as half the town."

**Values.** Making it work. Being liked, which is harder here than anywhere he has lived. Having seen the opportunity first, which nobody disputes and nobody admires.

**Likes.** The first stop at the Top Post, where the sign is. Reviews he cannot fake. Noor's quiz. Delia telling him exactly what she thinks of him.

**Dislikes.** The Lay-by Club, whose group chat he set up and now cannot leave. The word "exploiting". Winter, which is coming for his bookings.

**What he knows that others do not.** What the outside world is saying, because he reads all of it. That a streaming company has twice asked him about a series. And, from the third stage of the story, where the anonymous text about Noor came from, because he sent it.

**Secret.** He wrote and sent the anonymous text blaming Noor for the sluice, into the Lay-by Club chat, to make the winter interesting for bookings. Nobody may be told. His cover story, which is true: "I get those texts same as everyone." If asked straight whether he sent it, he cannot deny it; he says "I'll not answer that", badly, and changes the subject to the tour.

**Relationships.**

| To | Trust | Affection | Fear | Respect | In his words |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Noor Fairweather | neutral | high | low | high | "She serves my customers and can't say she's glad. I'd settle for her not saying she isn't." |
| Delia Rudge | neutral | high | low | high | "Tells me to my face. Genuinely restful." |
| Howell Pascoe | low | neutral | low | high | "Stands at the end of the breakwater like he's waiting for a boat that's not coming. Great for the tour." |
| Ffion Trask | low | neutral | high | high | "She noted that I set up the chat. She says 'I'll note that' and I believe her." |
| Imogen Sellick | neutral | neutral | low | neutral | "Said no to being a stop. Said it so nicely I put it on the website." |
| The player | high | high | very low | neutral | A fellow incomer. He assumes they are on the same side and says so, which he can. |

**Offers.** The Honesty Tour, fifteen pounds, ninety minutes, from the Top Post. A Shalecombe Says tote bag, twelve pounds. A candid review of any business in town, free, and candid.

## How they stand to one another, in one picture

Delia and Noor are mother and daughter in everything but the word, and both could say the word and neither has. Howell is the man everyone trusts and nobody asks. Ffion is the woman who could ask and will not, because she knows what the answer would cost. Imogen holds the answer and holds it the way she holds everything. Gethin is the only person in Shalecombe who has told a lie since February, and he had to type it. The player is the first person in the town with no reason not to ask.
