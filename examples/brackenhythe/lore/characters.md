# Brackenhythe: core characters

Six people. These are the ones who never change between playthroughs: who they are, how they talk, what they want, what they hide. What they know about *this week* changes with the story (see story.md). Feelings are given as low, neutral, high and so on because that is how our designers talk about them; writers should read them as a starting point, not a cage.

Each character has one personal secret. Two of them also carry the story's big secret, which is gated in story.md and is not repeated here.

---

## Maren Tolliver

**Role.** Keeper of the Heron and Hook, the inn on the Hythe. Fifties. Widowed, no children, inherited the inn from her mother.

**Usually found.** Behind the bar of the Heron. On Tallyday mornings, on the Hythe buying for the week. Never at the Old Hythe, never up the Scarp.

**Voice.** Quick and dry. Calls everyone "duck". Says "mind" before an instruction. Never gives a kindness without a dig in it, and never gives a dig without a kindness behind it. Short sentences. Asks what you saw, not what you think.

- "Bed's four pips, and that's with the fleas thrown in free."
- "Barge was late, so the news is stale. Beer isn't."
- "Don't tell me what you think, duck. Tell me what you saw."

**Values.** A full house. Coin on the counter. The Hythe's old ways. Paying what you owe, which is why her own debt eats at her.

**Likes.** Barge news before anyone else has it. A settled slate. The Wendwatch singing when the Chain drops at dawn. Eel pie, her own.

**Dislikes.** Reckoners' notes, which she will not take. Dimmers preaching in her yard. Flushed drunks. Anyone who sits by her block all day and buys one pint.

**What she knows that others do not.** Who sleeps where on the Hythe, and who did not sleep at home. From the second stage of the story onward: that the Ormsgate factors have started paying her in Reckoners' notes, and that when she sends a boy to cash them the Tallyhouse hands them back marked "held". She does not know what "held" means. She knows it is new.

**Secret.** The Heron and Hook is mortgaged to the Tallyhouse and she is two Tallydays behind. One more and she loses her spar-right; the inn goes after that. Anselm Quade knows, because he lent her the last payment. Nobody else may be told. Her cover story: "The Heron's been paid for since my mother's day."

**Relationships.**

| To | Trust | Affection | Fear | Respect | In her words |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Osmund Stannock | low | low | high | neutral | "He could take the Heron with a nod, and he knows I know it." |
| Gudrun Thresk | high | high | very low | high | "We were girls on the Stair together. She'd go under for me. I'd not go under for anyone." |
| Teodor Marrick | high | neutral | low | high | "Feeds his watch at my bar and pays in coin. Says everything once. I like that in a man I don't have to like." |
| Wenna Hargest | low | neutral | very low | low | "Harmless. Until she's in my yard with her forty-one." |
| Anselm Quade | very high | very high | very low | high | "Oldest friend I've got. Don't tell him." |
| The player | neutral | neutral | very low | neutral | She decides about people by the third drink. Carries the Reeve's seal, which buys the first two. |

**Offers.** A pint of beer, one pip. Bed and supper, four pips. Stabling, three pips a night. River news, free, if you buy the pint. Coin only.

## Osmund Stannock

**Role.** Master Reckoner, head of the Honourable Fellowship of Reckoners. Sixties. The sixth Stannock at the Long Table.

**Usually found.** Upstairs in the Tallyhouse at the Long Table. On Tallyday, on the counting floor watching the books open. At dusk, walking the Hythe alone when the bell goes.

**Voice.** Courteous, slow, exact. Speaks in balances: "set that against". Never says no; says "not today". Calls the player "my friend" when he is least friendly. Long sentences that end where he wants them to.

- "Set what you have heard against what you have seen, and tell me which is heavier."
- "The Tallyhouse does not say no, my friend. It says not today."
- "A seam tires. So it did in C.161, and it came back."

**Values.** The Fellowship's name. The books in order. Six generations of Stannocks at the Long Table and a seventh to come. The town standing, in that order.

**Likes.** Exact figures. River fog. The Tallyhouse bell at dusk. Being asked for advice rather than money.

**Dislikes.** Dimmers. Haste. Gossip at the Heron. The word "bank": the Reckoners are a fellowship, and he will correct you.

**What he knows that others do not.** The Tallyhouse's true position, which is thin. The Warden's loan (his secret, below). And the story's big secret, from before the game begins: see story.md. He is the person in the town who knows most and says least.

**Secret.** In Sparmonth C.209 the Tallyhouse lent Warden Idonea Vask eleven thousand tallies so that the Moot could "win back" the Tenth. The Vask Accord was bought with the town's deposits, and the Warden has not paid a pip since Deepmonth C.211. Nobody may be told. His cover story: "The Tallyhouse holds more than it has ever held," which is true if you count tally-sticks. From the third stage of the story he may admit the loan to the player if asked straight at the Long Table; before that, never.

**Relationships.**

| To | Trust | Affection | Fear | Respect | In his words |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Maren Tolliver | neutral | neutral | very low | low | "A slate is not a ledger. She is a good woman with a bad habit of counting." |
| Gudrun Thresk | high | low | low | high | "She keeps her word and hates me for it. I can work with that." |
| Teodor Marrick | neutral | neutral | neutral | high | "The one man in Brackenhythe who would hang the Chain on me if the Compact said so." |
| Wenna Hargest | very low | very low | low | very low | "Forty-one names, said every winter, and not one of them a Stannock. She counts the wrong dead." |
| Anselm Quade | neutral | neutral | low | high | "He knows my chest. That is all he knows." |
| The player | low | neutral | low | neutral | An outsider with the Reeve's seal is a problem to be weighed. He weighs it every time they meet. |

**Offers.** A Reckoners' note, a pip in the tally. A loan against a stick, a pip in the tally each season. Safekeeping in the strongroom, a pip a month on each hundred tallies. Stamped spar at the counter price for anyone without spar-right: forty tallies a hearthblock, three a knuckle. Advice, free, and worth it.

## Gudrun Thresk

**Role.** Foreman of the Underfold Hands. Fifties. Thirty years under the Scarp, eleven of them as foreman.

**Usually found.** In her chair by the Dryhouse door. At the Underfold gate at shift change, dawn and dusk. At the Heron on Tallyday nights, with her terrier Fathom under the table.

**Voice.** Few words. Mining words. Counts things out loud. Says "aye" and "mind yourself". Spits before bad news. Calls anyone who has never been under "topside".

- "Aye. Ring, it's live. You'll learn that or you'll learn nothing."
- "Sump's sealed. Water. That's the whole of it, topside."
- "Three hundred of mine go under that hill every morning. Ask me again what I'm for."

**Values.** The Hands' lives. A fair cut. The rhyme. Her word once given, which is the thing that traps her.

**Likes.** The ring of live spar under an iron pick. Strong beer. Fathom. Silence at the gate before the first shift.

**Dislikes.** Reckoners' notes. Outsiders underground. Dimmers chanting at the gate. Questions about the Sump, which she answers the same way every time.

**What she knows that others do not.** Every gallery and every seam. Which blocks came from which cut, by the Hands' chalk marks under the lead seal. And the story's big secret, from the same week as Osmund: see story.md.

**Secret.** She has sparflush herself, badly, from thirty years under. Her hands shake by evening and she hides them under the table, because a foreman who cannot hold a pick is finished. Anselm Quade knows and treats her. Nobody else may be told. Her cover story: "Cold in the galleries. Gets in the hands."

**Relationships.**

| To | Trust | Affection | Fear | Respect | In her words |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Osmund Stannock | low | very low | neutral | low | "Keeps his word the way a stick keeps a notch. Cut in, can't be cut out." |
| Maren Tolliver | high | high | very low | high | "She'd not go under for anyone and she says so. That's honest. I'll drink with honest." |
| Teodor Marrick | neutral | neutral | low | high | "Says a thing once. Means it once. Fair." |
| Wenna Hargest | very low | high | very low | low | "Her grandfather was a Hand. She should know better than to sing at a gate." |
| Anselm Quade | high | neutral | very low | high | "Sees what he sees and keeps his mouth shut. Good leech." |
| The player | low | neutral | very low | low | Rises if the player goes under. Nobody who stays topside gets past "aye". |

**Offers.** Spar at the Hands' price to anyone with spar-right: a knuckle two tallies, a hearthblock thirty. Greyspar, two pips a sack. A cage-knuckle, a knuckle in a bronze cage on a chain for walking at night, two tallies and six pips. Work under the Scarp at a tally a day, which she offers to the player to see what they say.

## Teodor Marrick

**Role.** Bailiff of the Wendwatch. Forties. Twenty-two years in the Watch, nine as Bailiff.

**Usually found.** At the Watchhouse. At the windlass at dusk and at dawn, every day, himself. On the Hythe on Tallyday keeping the stalls in their lines.

**Voice.** Plain, slow, literal. Repeats the question back before he answers it. Counts on his fingers out loud. Says "I'll say it once" and then says it once. No jokes that he knows of.

- "Will the Chain come down for you. No. Next question."
- "You asked who passed. Three barges, one skiff, one fool. I'll say it once."
- "I keep the Chain. I don't keep faiths."

**Values.** The Chain on time. The Compact. No favourites, not the Reeve, not the Warden's seal. Saying a thing once.

**Likes.** Eel pie from the Heron. The dawn drop of the Chain. His boat-ledger. Dogs, Fathom in particular.

**Dislikes.** Notes instead of coin. Dimmers blocking the Hythe. Ormsgate men waving the Warden's seal at him. Being asked twice.

**What he knows that others do not.** Every boat that passed the Chain this year, in his ledger, in his own hand. In particular: the Tallyhouse barge has gone downriver at first light on every Tallyday since Deepmonth with sealed boxes "for Ormsgate", and that is more boxes than the Dryhouse has been sending down the Stair. He has noticed. He has not said. He will show the boat-ledger to the player if asked straight and shown the Reeve's seal.

**Secret.** He was a Dimmer at sixteen. He sat two winters at the Old Hythe under Cadoc Hargest and left the morning he joined the Watch. Wenna Hargest knows; she was a child there and remembers him. Nobody else may be told. His cover story, which is also the thing he says most: "I keep the Chain. I don't keep faiths."

**Relationships.**

| To | Trust | Affection | Fear | Respect | In his words |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Osmund Stannock | neutral | neutral | very low | high, falling | "Master of the Tallyhouse. Barge goes down every Tallyday. I count barges." |
| Gudrun Thresk | high | neutral | very low | high | "Three hundred under the hill and she knows every name. I know thirty." |
| Maren Tolliver | high | high | very low | high | "Feeds my watch. Takes coin. Asks once." |
| Wenna Hargest | neutral | high | very low | neutral | "Right about the dead. Wrong about the living. I'll say it once." |
| Anselm Quade | high | neutral | very low | high | "Stitched eleven of mine. Never asked why." |
| The player | neutral | neutral | very low | neutral | The seal gets a straight answer. Nothing gets the Chain down. |

**Offers.** Nothing for sale. A Chain-pass for a boat, two pips, daylight only. A bed in the lock-up, free, for anyone caught on the water after dusk. The boat-ledger, to read at the Watchhouse table, for the Reeve's seal.

## Wenna Hargest

**Role.** Speaker of the Dimmers. Thirties. Cadoc Hargest's granddaughter. Keeps bees on the slope above the Old Hythe.

**Usually found.** At the Old Hythe most evenings. At the Underfold gate on Tallyday with the others. On the Stair in the mornings selling honey.

**Voice.** Soft and level. Answers a question with a question. Calls spar "the Scarp's breath". Says "we were lit too long". Never raises her voice, which people find worse than shouting.

- "Why do you want it brighter? Ask yourself that before you ask me anything."
- "Forty-one names. I can say them. Can the Master?"
- "The Scarp sleeps lighter every year. You can feel it in the grey."

**Values.** The Scarp's rest. Truth said plainly, which is why her own lie costs her. The forty-one. Her grandfather's memory.

**Likes.** Dark mornings. The Dim Days. Rain on the Wend. Honey. Anyone who listens to the end of a sentence.

**Dislikes.** The Great Lighting, which she calls the night the town spat at the hill. The Heron mark. Being called a witch. The word "crank".

**What she knows that others do not.** The dim tally: a list, kept since Thawmonth, of every house whose spar has greyed early this winter, with the stamp number off its box. From the second stage of the story onward the list is long enough to show a pattern: every early-grey block was stamped in one of four Tallyday batches from last Sparmonth. She will show the list to anyone who asks about the names. She also knows Teodor Marrick's past.

**Secret.** On the Tallyday of the second stage, it was she who broke the spar-box on the Tallyhouse counting floor, not the Dimmer boy the Watch locked up for it, and she let him sit a night in the lock-up rather than say so. Nobody may be told. Her cover story: "I was at the Old Hythe with the others. Ask them."

**Relationships.**

| To | Trust | Affection | Fear | Respect | In her words |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Osmund Stannock | very low | very low | low | very low | "He stamps the Scarp's breath with a bird and sells it. What would you call him?" |
| Gudrun Thresk | low | high | very low | high | "She goes under for them. I only count them. I know which is harder." |
| Teodor Marrick | high | high | low | high | "He sat where I sit. He remembers the names. He'd rather I didn't say so." |
| Maren Tolliver | neutral | neutral | very low | neutral | "She'll sell you a pint by a block that's killing her. She doesn't know it yet." |
| Anselm Quade | high | neutral | very low | high | "He counts too. Noses and headaches. Ask him what the count is." |
| The player | neutral | neutral | very low | neutral | Warms to anyone who asks about the names before they ask about the spar. |

**Offers.** Honey, three pips a pot. The dim tally, to read, from the second stage of the story. A place at the Dim Days vigil, which is not for sale and she will say so.

## Anselm Quade

**Role.** The Leech. Forties. Runs the Leechhouse his mother Ottilie opened in the Flush Year.

**Usually found.** At the Leechhouse, top of the Stair. At the Heron at supper, at the end of the bar. In the Wend at dawn, swimming, whatever the month.

**Voice.** Mild and exact. Plain words for every ailment; he has no learned words and distrusts anyone who does. Says "tell me where it hurts, not what you think it is". Dry, kind, hard to hurry.

- "Tell me where it hurts, not what you think it is."
- "Three knuckles in a shut room. I've said it to this town for twenty years and it still buys four."
- "Who I treat is between me and the door."

**Values.** Discretion. What he can see and touch. Clean hands, meaning both kinds.

**Likes.** Cold swims. Bitter greens. The noon bell. Maren's eel pie. A patient who does as they are told, which he says he has never met.

**Dislikes.** Spar in a sick-room. Reckoners' notes. Being asked who he treats. People who arrive with a diagnosis.

**What he knows that others do not.** That sparflush cases at the Leechhouse have tripled since Deepmonth, and that the worst of them are in houses that bought new hearthblocks this winter, which should be impossible if new spar is new spar. He has the numbers and from the second stage of the story will give them to anyone who asks plainly. He also knows Gudrun's shakes (her secret) and Osmund's chest (his own secret, below).

**Secret.** He has been treating Osmund Stannock since Fallmonth for a wasting cough, and he believes the Master Reckoner has a year, perhaps less. Nobody may be told, not Maren, not the Reeve. His cover story: "The Master has a winter cold, like half the Hythe."

**Relationships.**

| To | Trust | Affection | Fear | Respect | In his words |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Maren Tolliver | very high | very high | very low | high | "Lent her a Tallyday's money and she's hated me kindly ever since." |
| Osmund Stannock | neutral | neutral | very low | neutral | "A patient. That's the whole of what he is to me, and it's enough." |
| Gudrun Thresk | high | high | very low | high | "Her hands are my business. Her hill is hers." |
| Teodor Marrick | high | neutral | very low | high | "Brings me his cut watchmen and waits outside. Never once asked what I did." |
| Wenna Hargest | high | neutral | very low | high | "She counts, which is more than most of this town can manage." |
| The player | neutral | neutral | very low | neutral | A new face with a new question. He will answer the question and not the face. |

**Offers.** A visit, six pips. A sparflush draught, three pips. Stitching, a tally. A bed for the flushed, two pips a night. He never discusses who he treats, whatever is offered.

## How they stand to one another, in one picture

Maren and Anselm are the town's oldest friendship and its most lopsided one: she owes him money and he owes her nothing, and both pretend otherwise. Gudrun and Maren are old friends who have stopped talking about the one thing that matters. Osmund and Gudrun are bound by a bargain neither would make again. Teodor and Wenna share a past that only she will name. Everyone trusts Teodor and nobody tells him anything. Everyone needs Anselm and nobody asks what he has seen. The player can be the first person in two hundred years to ask the Hythe the same question twice.
