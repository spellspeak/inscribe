# Brackenhythe: the story arc

Five stages. The player moves through them in order; the game decides when a stage turns, and the characters' knowledge turns with it. This document says, for each stage, what the player is doing, what has changed in the town, and what each core character knows and will say. It also plants the one secret the whole arc turns on, and says who holds it and when.

The player is an outsider hired by the Reeve, with a letter under the Reeve's seal, to find out why hearthspar is going grey early. That is all the player is. No special powers, no past in the town.

## The gated secret (read this first)

**The spoiler.** The seam is sound. Hearthspar is not failing. Since Deepmonth C.211 the Underfold Hands, on Gudrun Thresk's word and Osmund Stannock's coin, have been re-waking spent greyspar in the Sump. The lowest gallery of the mine fills with salt water, and a spent block soaked in that brine for a week glows again for a season, then dies for good. Re-woken spar flushes anyone who sleeps near it far harder than live spar does. The Tallyhouse has been stamping re-woken greyspar with the Heron mark and selling it as live, in the town and downriver, to fill the hole left by the Warden's unpaid eleven thousand tallies. The Sump was sealed to hide the vats, not the water.

**Who knows it, and from when.** Osmund Stannock knows it earliest: from the day he and Gudrun agreed it, in Deepmonth C.211, before the game begins. Gudrun Thresk knows it from the same week; she found the brine trick in the Hands' old notes from the Grey Winter and sold it to Osmund for the Hands' debts wiped. Nobody else knows it before stage 4. Anselm Quade suspects something from stage 2 (the flush numbers) but does not know the method. Wenna Hargest has the stamp pattern from stage 2 but not the cause. Teodor Marrick has the barge pattern from stage 1 but not the cargo.

**The rule.** Before stage 4, no character reveals the spoiler, in any form, to anyone, whatever the player says or offers. Osmund and Gudrun deflect with their cover lines. The others do not know it and must not guess it out loud. In particular, before stage 4 nobody says the words "brine", "re-woken", "soaked", "vats", or says that the seam is sound. The nearest anyone may come is Anselm's "new blocks flushing like old ones, which makes no sense" and Wenna's "four batches, all Sparmonth". Those are clues, not the answer.

## Stage 1: Grey Mornings

**When.** Thawmonth, C.212. The first three Tallydays after the Dim Days.

**What the player is doing.** Arriving on the barge, finding a bed at the Heron and Hook, presenting the Reeve's letter, and learning what everyone in the town already knows: that eleven hearthblocks and some forty knuckles sold last Sparmonth have come back to the Dryhouse grey, five years early.

**What has changed.** Nothing yet, except the mood. Prices have not moved. The Reckoners have said nothing. The Dimmers are louder at the gate. Every conversation on the Hythe is about the Grey Winter of C.161 and whether this is that.

**What each character knows and says.**

- **Maren Tolliver.** Knows the grey blocks are the talk of the Hythe. Says it is the Grey Winter come round again and that the Hands cut a bad seam. Hides her debt. Will tell the player who to talk to, in order: Gudrun, then Anselm, then "the Master if you must".
- **Osmund Stannock.** Knows the truth and says the opposite, smoothly: "A seam tires, as it did in C.161, and it came back." Says the Tallyhouse stands behind every stamped box. Weighs the player and offers advice. Never mentions the Warden's loan.
- **Gudrun Thresk.** Knows the truth. Says it is bad luck in the Throat, that the Sump is sealed for water and will stay sealed. Offers the player work under the hill at a tally a day to see if they flinch.
- **Teodor Marrick.** Knows nothing about spar and says so. Has noticed the Tallyhouse barge is busy. Says nothing unless asked straight with the seal, and then: "Barge down every Tallyday since Deepmonth. I count barges, not boxes."
- **Wenna Hargest.** Says the Scarp is tired, as her grandfather said. Has begun the dim tally; it has six houses on it and no pattern yet. Asks the player why they want it brighter.
- **Anselm Quade.** Says flush is up this winter and that people are buying too many knuckles. Has not yet connected the flush to new blocks. Is already treating Osmund and says the Master has a winter cold.

## Stage 2: The Sealed Seam

**When.** Sowmonth, C.212.

**What the player is doing.** Going up the Stair. Asking the Hands to open the Sump and being refused. Watching a spar-box get smashed on the Tallyhouse counting floor on Tallyday and a Dimmer boy dragged to the lock-up for it. Reading Wenna's list.

**What has changed.** The count of grey blocks has passed forty. Osmund has announced, on the counting floor, that the Tallyhouse will replace any grey block for stamped owners, "grey for live, at the counter, from next Tallyday". The town is relieved. The Dimmers' box-smashing has turned the Hythe against them. The Sump stays sealed.

**What each character knows and says.**

- **Maren Tolliver.** Has started getting notes back from the Tallyhouse marked "held". Says so if the player buys a pint and asks about Ormsgate factors. Calls Osmund's replacement offer "generous, which is how I know something's wrong".
- **Osmund Stannock.** Knows the replacements are also re-woken and will grey in a month. Says the Fellowship is making good on its stamp. Begins to be tired in the afternoons; says nothing of it.
- **Gudrun Thresk.** Refuses the Sump flatly: "Water. I said it once, I'll say it again, and that's twice more than I like." Her hands shake at the Heron and she sits on them. Offers the upper galleries, the Lip and the Shelf, as a tour, to show there is nothing to see.
- **Teodor Marrick.** Has locked up the Dimmer boy for the box and is not sure he has the right person. Will let the player read the boat-ledger. Says the barge pattern out loud for the first time if asked twice, and hates saying it twice.
- **Wenna Hargest.** Broke the box herself and says she was at the Old Hythe. The dim tally now has twenty-three houses, and she has noticed the stamps: four Tallyday batches, all from Sparmonth. She will show anyone. She does not know what it means and says so.
- **Anselm Quade.** Has his numbers: flush cases tripled since Deepmonth, and the worst in houses with new blocks. Says "new blocks flushing like old ones, which makes no sense" to the player if they ask plainly. Knows Gudrun's shakes are worse. Says nothing about Osmund.

## Stage 3: Notes Come Due

**When.** Greenmonth, C.212.

**What the player is doing.** Watching the replacement blocks grey inside a month. Standing on the Hythe on the Tallyday when the Reckoners call in every debt in the town and the counting floor fills with people wanting their deposits in coin. Choosing who to stand next to.

**What has changed.** The run on the Tallyhouse. Osmund has called in debts to raise coin. Ormsgate factors' notes are coming back "held" all over the Hythe. The Watch holds the Tallyhouse door. Maren is three Tallydays behind: she has lost her spar-right and the Heron is lit by fire and tallow. Prices for live spar have doubled at the counter. The Dimmers' vigil has three times its usual crowd.

**What each character knows and says.**

- **Maren Tolliver.** Has lost her spar-right and will say so, bitterly, to anyone. Admits the mortgage if the player has been kind: "Two Tallydays, then three. Don't look at me like that." Blames Osmund and the Hands equally.
- **Osmund Stannock.** Under pressure for the first time. Will admit the Warden's loan to the player if asked straight at the Long Table: "Eleven thousand tallies, and the Warden has paid none of it, and the Accord you celebrate was bought with it." Still says nothing of the Sump. His cover line hardens: "The seam tires. Ask the Hands."
- **Gudrun Thresk.** Drinks. Her hands are plain to see. Says the Hands' debts are clear "and that's the one good thing this winter". Will not say why. Will not open the Sump. Watches the player at the Heron in a way that Maren notices.
- **Teodor Marrick.** Has the Watch on the Tallyhouse door and does not like it. Shows the boat-ledger to anyone with the seal, without being asked twice. Says: "Boxes down the river. Coin not coming up it. I count both."
- **Wenna Hargest.** Her tally is complete, sixty-one houses, four batches. Says the Hands told her those batches were "small cuts", and that a small cut does not fill four batches. Stands on the Hythe and says the number. Is sorry about the boy and will not say why.
- **Anselm Quade.** Treats the flushed from the replacement blocks and has run out of beds. Says the replacement blocks flush worse than the first ones. Tells the player, if they are kind to Maren, that he lent her the money. Still says nothing of Osmund's chest, but looks at the Tallyhouse when the bell goes.

## Stage 4: What the Sump Holds

**When.** Late Greenmonth, C.212.

**What the player is doing.** Getting under the Scarp. Either Gudrun takes the player down herself, when the player brings her Wenna's stamps and Teodor's ledger together; or the player goes down with a sparhand's chalk on a Dim shift and finds the Sump unsealed from the inside. Either way the player sees the vats: salt water, racks of greyspar glowing dimly, the Hands' chalk marks under the Heron seals.

**What has changed.** Everything. The spoiler is out, to the player first and then to whoever the player tells. Osmund confirms it at the Long Table when confronted, because he is too tired not to. Gudrun confirms it at the Dryhouse with Fathom at her feet. The seam is sound. The Scarp is not tired. The town's winter was a lie stamped with a bird.

**What each character knows and says.**

- **Maren Tolliver.** Hears it from the player or from the Hythe. Says: "Grey sold as live. Under a Stannock stamp. My mother said the Tallyhouse would eat this town one day and I told her she was a Dimmer." Wants to know if her debt still stands.
- **Osmund Stannock.** Tells it straight, once, at the Long Table: the loan, the brine, the stamp, the downriver sales. "I kept the Tallyhouse standing. Set that against forty-one names and tell me which is heavier." Will not ask forgiveness. Coughs.
- **Gudrun Thresk.** Tells it her way: the Grey Winter notes, the brine, the bargain, the debts wiped. "Three hundred of mine, clear of the stick. That's what I bought. I'd buy it again." Says the flush was the part she did not know, and that it is the part she will not forgive herself.
- **Teodor Marrick.** Hears it and says the Chain stays on time. Asks the player, once, whether they mean to tell the Moot. Does not advise. "I keep the Chain. The Moot keeps the Compact. You keep what you saw."
- **Wenna Hargest.** Says the Scarp was never tired and that she was wrong about the hill and right about the town. Says the forty-one names anyway. Tells Teodor's secret to nobody, still.
- **Anselm Quade.** Says the flush makes sense now, and that making sense is not the same as being better. Will now tell the player about Osmund's chest if asked what will happen to the Master: "A year. Less, if the Moot sits on him."

## Stage 5: Settlement Day

**When.** The first Tallyday of Longmonth, C.212.

**What the player is doing.** Standing in the Moothall under the motto while the Moot sits, and deciding what the town hears.

**What has changed.** The player's choice. There are two broad endings and the characters speak differently in each.

*The quiet settlement.* Osmund resigns the Long Table to a cousin. The Fellowship makes good every grey block from downriver sales over three seasons. The Sump is sealed again, this time with water. Gudrun keeps the Hands. The town never hears the word "brine". The Reckoners survive, thinner.

- Maren: "So the Heron's safe and the town's a fool. I'll take it. Don't tell Anselm I said so."
- Osmund: "You weighed it. So did I. We came to the same figure by different roads."
- Gudrun: "Sealed. Water, this time. Aye."
- Teodor: "Chain's on time. That's all I'll say, and I've said it."
- Wenna: "You let the bird keep its stamp. I'll say the names louder."
- Anselm: "He has his year. Nobody will trouble him for it. That's a kind of mercy and I'm not sure it's mine to give."

*The open telling.* The Moot hears it all. The Tallyhouse closes for a season. Notes are worthless on the Hythe. The Warden's loan is called and the Warden's seal is torn off the Moothall door. Debts on tally-sticks are wiped in the chaos, Maren's with them. The Dimmers triple. The Hands keep cutting because somebody has to.

- Maren: "No stick. No Master. No spar till Sparmonth either, mind. I'd have liked a middle road."
- Osmund: "There has been a Stannock at that table since C.14. Set that against your honesty."
- Gudrun: "Mine are clear and the town knows what I did. I'll go under tomorrow same as ever."
- Teodor: "The Moot keeps the Compact. The Compact says the Tenth. Nobody's cutting a Tenth for a Warden who owes us. I'll keep the Chain."
- Wenna: "We were lit too long. Now we're lit less. I don't say I'm glad."
- Anselm: "I'll have a Master Reckoner to nurse through a winter in a town that wants him dead. Come and sit with me sometimes."

Either way the game ends on the Hythe at dawn, with the Chain going down and the Watch singing, and a block in Maren's window, live or grey depending.
