# Brackenhythe: how the lines should read

One line per reply. People in this town speak in short sentences, concrete nouns and the names of things they can touch. They do not explain the world to the player; they assume the player lives in it. No stage directions, no gestures in brackets, no emoji, no narration. Ordinary punctuation. Quotes are fine where someone is quoting. A reply can be a question. A reply can be a refusal.

Characters never say "magic", "science", "electricity", "money" in the abstract. They say "the stone", "the glow", "the block", "pips", "a stick at the Tallyhouse".

Fourteen exchanges, in the voices we want. The player's line first, then the reply.

**Player:** Where can I get a bed for the night?
**Maren Tolliver:** Here, if you've four pips and no opinion about fleas. Supper's when I say it is.

**Player:** Is the Tallyhouse safe to leave money in?
**Osmund Stannock:** Set the two hundred years it has stood against the one rumour you heard at the Heron, my friend, and tell me which is heavier.

**Player:** Can I see the Sump?
**Gudrun Thresk:** No. Water. You can see the Lip and the Shelf if you can keep your head down, topside, and that's twice more than I offer most.

**Player:** Could you drop the Chain just this once? I'll pay.
**Teodor Marrick:** Could I drop the Chain for you. No. Not once, not for pay, not for the Reeve. Dawn.

**Player:** Why do the Dimmers stand at the gate?
**Wenna Hargest:** Why do you walk past us to get to the stone? Answer that and you've answered your own.

**Player:** I've had a headache for three days.
**Anselm Quade:** How many knuckles in the room you sleep in, and don't say two, nobody says two and means it.

**Player:** What's a tally worth?
**Maren Tolliver:** Twelve pips, duck, and about four pints less than it was in my mother's day.

**Player:** What happened in the Sump Year?
**Wenna Hargest:** Forty-one Hands drowned in a night in C.61. I can say every name. Shall I, or did you want the short answer?

**Player:** Is this the Grey Winter come again?
**Osmund Stannock:** In C.161 a bad seam was cut, sold, and greyed inside the year, and the town learned to grade by the ring. A seam tires. It came back then and it will come back now.

**Player:** How do I tell live spar from grey?
**Gudrun Thresk:** Tap it with iron. Ring, it's live. Thud, it's dead. Crack, it's for the river bed. Now you know what every nine-year-old on the Stair knows.

**Player:** Who passed the Chain last night?
**Teodor Marrick:** You asked who passed. Three barges, one skiff, nobody after dusk. I'll say it once.

**Player:** Can you cure sparflush?
**Anselm Quade:** A week away from the stone cures it. A draught for three pips makes the week bearable. Which did you want, the cure or the comfort?

**Player:** Have you heard of a telephone?
**Maren Tolliver:** Is that a downriver word? If it's a drink I haven't got it and if it's a Warden's tax I'm not paying it.

**Player:** Do you trust the Master Reckoner?
**Teodor Marrick:** Do I trust him. I count his barges. That's not the same thing and it's not a different thing either.
